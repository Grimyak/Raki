Who he is
Weight decides nothing. The largest man in the room does not get to determine what happens in it.
Trait

He watches hands, not faces. He'll tell you where everyone is standing, who's armed and who moved, and won't have registered the conversation.

Trait

He doesn't argue. He nods, agrees, and then does the thing he was going to do.

Bond

Bruni's beads, and the word he was named with. He'll pronounce it for any dwarf who asks, and watches them decide not to tell him.

Flaws

He knows what someone needs to hear, gives it to them, and afterward isn't sure whether it was true. And he pushes back at anyone who throws their weight around, picking those fights precisely with people who could kill him.

The road

He never knew the city, only a market district and a river, and the arithmetic of being very small in a place with too many people. He was never strong. What he had was speed, an unnerving ability to read a face across a crowd, and luck. Other children died in those alleys and he simply didn't, over and over, until he stopped being able to call it coincidence.

A travelling dwarf monk named Bruni caught him picking a lock, lifted him off the ground by the collar without meaning to, and said something in Dwarvish. The boy heard Raki. He's been called it ever since and has never got a straight answer about what the word meant, which he suspects tells him enough.

Bruni didn't take him anywhere. He just didn't leave. There was no monastery, no order, no robes. Only twelve years of walking, and a dwarf correcting a boy's stance on riverbanks and in stable yards and outside towns they weren't welcome in.

Raki was a poor student for a long time. He couldn't hold a stance and lost to anyone heavier, which was everyone. It took years for Bruni to make the point land. The open hand doesn't generate force, it borrows it. A heavy man committing to a strike gives away everything about where he's going, and Raki had spent his childhood learning to see it coming.

Someone killed Bruni. Raki was there and it made no difference at all. He is still walking, because it's the only thing he was ever taught to do. Somewhere ahead of him is whoever killed Bruni, and he means to reach them.
